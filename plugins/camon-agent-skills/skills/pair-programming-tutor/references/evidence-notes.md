# Evidence notes

This skill adapts learning and scientific-practice ideas into a software-tutoring workflow. They justify useful defaults; they do not guarantee learning outcomes for every learner or prove claims about a learner's brain.

- Vygotsky, L. S. (1978). *Mind in Society*. The zone of proximal development and scaffolding motivate short diagnostic questions and a next-step curriculum.
- Roediger, H. L., & Karpicke, J. D. (2006). Test-enhanced learning: Taking memory tests improves long-term retention. *Psychological Science*, 17(3), 249–255. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x. This supports retrieval practice; it does not report a universal “three times stronger synaptic connection” result.
- Popper, K. (1959). *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*. Falsifiable predictions and comparison conditions motivate the laboratory section.
- Collins, A., Brown, J. S., & Newman, S. E. (1989). Cognitive apprenticeship. In *Knowing, Learning, and Instruction*. Modeling and coached practice motivate the debugging clinic.
- Hutchins, E. (1995). *Cognition in the Wild*. External representations motivate optional notes and glossary artifacts.
- Cepeda, N. J., Pashler, H., Vul, E., Wixted, J. T., & Rohrer, D. (2006). Distributed practice in verbal recall tasks. *Psychological Science*, 17(11), 1095–1102. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2006.01846.x. This supports revisiting retained learning records over time.

The attached rationale is preserved as the author-provided explanation. The specific numerical claim in its retrieval-practice section should be treated as illustrative wording rather than a result established by the cited 2006 paper.
